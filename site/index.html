The story

One Tuesday. Everything he built.

Myko Ubuntu built his life around four things: his martial arts students, the woman who'd stood beside him since they were teenagers, his aging parents, and a three-word promise passed down through three generations: lead with love, tell the truth, demand justice.

Then his mother's heart started failing, and an algorithm took only a few seconds to decide that she wasn't worth saving.

What follows is one Tuesday that unravels everything: a denied insurance claim, a bank "assistance" program built to say no most of the time, and a stranger's cruelty at the exact wrong moment. By nightfall, the man who once taught children how to fall down safely is running from the same city he's spent his whole life trying to protect.

Once the system leaves you nothing left to lose, what do you actually owe the people who still love you?

As detectives close in and his family's world collapses in real time, Myko has to face a question no courtroom and no amount of love can answer cleanly.

And as a trial exposes exactly how many hands touched this tragedy before it ever reached Myko's, his sister starts asking a harder question than guilt or innocence: how many other families has this same machine already swallowed, and how many more will it take before someone makes it stop?

The author

Keith B. Still

Keith B. Still is the pseudonym of a storyteller who blends cinematic pacing with literary depth to explore love, truth, justice, and family, along with personal and collective responsibility. His characters are flawed yet deeply human, navigating a world where institutions often fail the very people they were built to serve.

Still began writing Myko's Reward after a question that refused to leave him alone: what does it actually cost an ordinary, decent person to keep every promise he's ever made, when the institutions around him are quietly built to make that impossible? The answer took three generations of one fictional family, and a story that refused to resolve as neatly as he first intended.

If this book stays with you, that's exactly the kind of conversation he hopes to keep having with readers, one hard question at a time.
